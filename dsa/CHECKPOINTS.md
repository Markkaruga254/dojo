# DSA checkpoints

Use these checkpoints after each topic block and at roadmap gates. The purpose is to test whether a pattern transfers to an unseen problem, not whether you remember a familiar solution.

## Per-topic exit check

Before marking a pattern as independent:

- [ ] Explain the pattern and invariant in plain language.
- [ ] State time and space complexity before coding.
- [ ] Solve one unseen easy and one unseen medium without hints.
- [ ] Test a normal case, a boundary case, and an adversarial case.
- [ ] Re-solve one previously missed problem from a blank file.
- [ ] Log duration, result, and independent / hinted / assisted status.

If you needed a hint, mark the problem honestly and schedule a cold re-solve in 3, 7, or 21 days.

## Roadmap gates

| Gate | Minimum evidence | If the gate is missed |
|---|---|---|
| G1 — end Week 4 | At least 40 logged attempts; at least 60% of easy/medium attempts independently solved; one mock retrospective | Revisit the two weakest patterns for 3–5 days, then run a new mixed set |
| G2 — end Week 8 | At least 90 attempts; trees, heaps, backtracking, and graphs each have at least 6 solved problems; mocks 2–3 reviewed | Reduce new-problem volume and focus on the weakest topic with cold re-solves |
| G3 — end Week 12 | At least 150 attempts; at least 20 spaced re-solves; timed set of 2 problems in 90 minutes with correct brute force for both and at least one fully passing | Practise the exact failure mode, then repeat with unseen problems |
| G4 — Weeks 13–16 | Last three mocks consistently meet the current rubric target; explain complexity and trade-offs aloud | Do not increase hours; replace low-priority work with a focused remediation loop |

These are programme targets, not guarantees of hiring readiness. Confirm any employer assessment format from the actual invitation.

## Mock protocol

1. Start a timer and use the permitted language/environment.
2. Restate the task and write examples before implementation.
3. Describe brute force and complexity.
4. Implement the best approach you can justify.
5. Test edge cases and explain the result aloud.
6. Record the first point of failure and the next remediation task.
